# Customer Discovery Protocol

## Hypotheses to test
- H1: Issuer Corsec teams regularly compare ≥2 corporate action scenarios manually.
- H2: Ownership reconciliation and evidence checking consume meaningful analyst time.
- H3: Traceable and reviewer-approved scenarios offer value beyond Datindo's existing services.
- H4: A standalone BYOD pilot is commercially acceptable without production integration.
- H5: A BAE can license/white-label a planner as a new service to issuers.

## Target interview segments
- A: BAE operations / registrar analyst, Datindo or other firms.
- B: Issuer Corporate Secretary.
- C: Corporate finance/legal advisor (controls/reviewer).
- D: Procurement/IT (data, deployment, vendor evaluation).

## Discovery questions (neutral; do not lead witnesses)
1. Describe the last time you evaluated a change to shareholder structure.
2. Which source documents were needed? Which fields were missing?
3. How did you confirm affiliated/controller holdings?
4. What tools are used today? What was difficult or error-prone?
5. How long did it take, split by data gathering, calculation, review, memo drafting?
6. How are rule changes reflected and historic results reproduced?
7. What cannot be shared with a third-party SaaS?
8. How would a successful limited pilot be measured?
9. Who owns the decision to try/buy a new tool? What review is required?
10. Is this recurring enough to justify a license, or only occasional advisory work?

## Decision gates
- Proceed to paid pilot discussions when at least one qualified lead confirms workflow pain and agrees to define an evaluation. This is a gate, not a forecast.
- Pivot feature if multiple users report they already solve it adequately.
- Do not treat email opens, website clicks, or friendly replies as purchase intent.
- Keep legal data rights separate from product interest; interest never grants access to private registers.
